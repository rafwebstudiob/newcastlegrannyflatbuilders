Mine subsidence and granny flats in Newcastle

In short: Parts of Newcastle are in proclaimed Mine Subsidence Districts. If your lot is in one, a new granny flat has to be designed to the Subsidence Advisory NSW guideline assigned to your property. For some guidelines, Subsidence Advisory itself must approve the plans before work starts.

City of Newcastle says parts of its area have been subject to underground coal mine workings or sit within the zone of influence of old workings. This guide summarises Subsidence Advisory NSW and City of Newcastle information, with links. It is not engineering, legal or planning advice.

Last updated: 26 November 2026

This page summarises official information so you know what to expect. It is not engineering, legal or planning advice. Your builder, a certifier, City of Newcastle or Subsidence Advisory NSW can confirm what applies to your lot.

What a Mine Subsidence District is

Mine Subsidence Districts are areas of NSW with a potential subsidence risk from planned, active or non-active underground coal mining. They are declared by Subsidence Advisory NSW, which regulates building and subdivision in them under the Coal Mine Subsidence Compensation Act 2017. The NSW Government's list of districts includes Newcastle and Killingworth-Wallsend, plus several Lake Macquarie districts nearby.

Sources: NSW Government, Mine Subsidence Districts; Newcastle DCP, Section B3 Mine subsidence.

Step 1: check your lot

Search your address or Lot/DP on the NSW Planning Portal Spatial Viewer. If the property is in a district, the search results show the Subsidence Advisory surface development guideline assigned to it. An "Underground Coal Mining Area" layer also shows whether coal has been extracted under or near the property. That layer includes a buffer around old workings and is updated about every three months.

Guidelines are assigned property by property, based on the type of mining and the predicted subsidence. Subsidence Advisory notes that neighbouring properties can have different guidelines, for example where one is mined beneath and the one next door is not. So don't rely on what applied to a neighbour.

Step 2: who approves the granny flat

Subsidence Advisory NSW says you must get its approval before starting to build or extend a home or structure in a district. How that happens depends on your property's guideline:

  • Guidelines 2, 3, 3A, 6 and 8: councils and accredited certifiers can approve applications that comply with the guideline, without referral to Subsidence Advisory.
  • Guidelines 1, 1A, 4, 5 and 7: Subsidence Advisory's approval is required.

Applications can be lodged directly with Subsidence Advisory through its online portal, or as integrated development through council, which refers them on. Subsidence Advisory says applications that comply with the guideline are approved within 7 calendar days of receipt. Designs that go beyond the guideline get a merit assessment, which is determined within 40 calendar days unless more information is requested. It may ask for things like geotechnical studies, and it can approve with conditions on building materials, construction methods, and the size, height and location of new structures.

Some minor works, such as small carports, decks and fences, don't need Subsidence Advisory approval. Check the exempt development list before assuming anything is exempt.

Source: Subsidence Advisory NSW, Development approval process.

City of Newcastle's requirements

The Newcastle Development Control Plan (Section B3) applies to any new building or structure, extension, structural alteration or subdivision on land in a Mine Subsidence District. It says mine subsidence must be considered in the preliminary design, and Subsidence Advisory approval should be sought before a development application is lodged. Where Subsidence Advisory requires it, the plans it has stamped and endorsed go in with the DA. Designs should follow the relevant guideline, with notes on the drawings confirming compliance.

That fits with the planning pathways in our NSW granny flat approvals guide, which covers complying development and DAs. Mine subsidence is an extra layer on top of those rules, not a replacement for them.

Shallow old workings and Guideline 1

Properties above shallow historical mine workings, with a high or moderate risk of a pothole (sinkhole) forming at the surface, are assigned Guideline 1. Subsidence Advisory says Guideline 1 allows up to two residential buildings of a described type, such as a single-storey brick veneer home or a two-storey timber or steel-framed home with lightweight cladding. It describes a standard sinkhole foundation design. Alternatively, a qualified structural engineer can design the building to cope with a pothole up to 5 m across forming anywhere underneath it. Designs that don't fit Guideline 1 go to a merit assessment.

Source: Subsidence Advisory NSW, Surface Development Guideline 1.

Does it add cost?

Subsidence Advisory says that in most cases its requirements for standard residential development don't increase construction costs and are consistent with the National Construction Code. Engineering design, extra reports or a merit assessment can still add steps, so ask your builder what your property's guideline means for the design and the quote.

Why the approval matters later

The NSW Government says all homes and buildings in NSW, inside or outside a district, are eligible for compensation if damaged by mine subsidence, provided the correct development approvals were obtained. When buying in a district, it suggests asking your solicitor or conveyancer to confirm that structures were built with the correct approvals.

Questions to ask a granny flat builder

  • Is my lot in a Mine Subsidence District, and which guideline does the Spatial Viewer show?
  • Does your standard design comply with that guideline, or will it need engineering or a merit assessment?
  • Who lodges the Subsidence Advisory application, and is that time and cost in the quote?
  • Will the stamped plans be ready before the DA or complying development certificate is lodged?
